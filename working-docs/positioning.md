# Positioning & Strategy

## Target Market

**"Serious redraft" leagues** — not casual ESPN users, not dynasty (yet).

These are redraft leagues that:

- Want scoring rules ESPN/Yahoo/Sleeper can't handle
- Have commissioners frustrated by platform limitations
- Are willing to pay for flexibility

## The Wedge

**Scoring engine flexibility.** The platform wins on:

| Feature                   | Why It Matters                             |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Median scoring            | High demand, ESPN doesn't support natively |
| Position-specific scoring | TE premium, superflex differentials        |
| Conditional bonuses       | 100-yard games, 40+ yard TDs               |
| IDP support               | ESPN's IDP is notoriously bad              |
| Clean UX                  | MFL has flexibility but terrible interface |

**Position:** "MFL's power, Sleeper's usability" — for leagues that outgrew ESPN.

**Tagline options:**

- "Your league's rules, not ESPN's"
- "Fantasy infrastructure for commissioners who need more"

## Why Redraft First

- **Simpler to build:** No taxi squads, rookie drafts, contract tracking, multi-year history
- **Lower switching friction:** Leagues reset yearly, less history to migrate
- **Faster iteration:** Clean slate every August, can break and fix quickly

## Realistic Expectations

| Year | Leagues     | Focus                          |
| ---- | ----------- | ------------------------------ |
| 1    | 50-150      | Validate that people will pay  |
| 2    | 200-600     | Word of mouth, retention proof |
| 3-5  | 1,000-3,000 | Scale if retention holds       |

## Expansion Path

1. **Now:** Nail scoring engine for serious redraft
2. **Soon:** Keepers (many serious redraft leagues use them, low complexity), then IDP (niche but strong differentiator)
3. **Later:** Full dynasty (taxi squads, rookie drafts, contracts) once core is proven
4. **Future:** Own the "leagues that need more" segment across formats

## What Lands Deals

- Solve a specific scoring problem they can't do elsewhere
- Make migration easy (import from ESPN/Sleeper/MFL)
- UX that leaguemates will actually use
- Target commissioners — they're the decision-maker

## What We're NOT

- Not competing for casual ESPN users (they won't pay)
- Not a standalone analytics subscription (that's FantasyPros) — but in-app data viz (projections with ranges, trend lines, variance) helps owners make better decisions
- Not dynasty-first (too complex to start)
